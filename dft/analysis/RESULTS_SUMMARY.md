#### On x — and a correction to the S06 framing

**x = 2 and Δn = +1 for all nine reactions, and this is not a free choice.** It is fixed by the
composition of the clusters that were actually computed: `[M(H₂O)₆]²⁺` holds six waters,
`[M(L)(H₂O)₄]^q` holds four, so two are released. **No `[M(L)(H₂O)₅]^q` species was ever built or
run**, so no x = 1 reaction can be evaluated from this job set.

> The S06 decision-log entry states that at P0 "Pb and Cu are matched with each other (both x = 1,
> Δn = 0)". That describes a reaction — `[M(H₂O)₆]²⁺ + LH₂ → [M(LH₂)(H₂O)₅]²⁺ + H₂O` — **for which
> no calculation exists**. What was computed is the four-water product in every case. The
> monodentate complexes are five-coordinate because the second galloyl oxygen dangles, not because a
> fifth water was retained.

**This is better for the argument than the S06 framing assumed.** Because all nine reactions have
identical stoichiometry, the standard-state term and the free energy of the released water are
**identical across all nine and cancel exactly in every ΔΔG**. There is no stoichiometric
contamination in any cross-metal difference.

What differs between a monodentate and a bidentate case is the **product's first-shell coordination
number** — 5 against 6. That is a chemical difference, and arguably it is itself the result, but it
is not a bookkeeping error and it does not invalidate the arithmetic.

`REACTIONS.md` §3's claim that "x = 2, identically, for all three metals and all three protonation
states" is therefore **correct as a stoichiometric statement** and **wrong if read as a claim about
denticity**. §3.2 of that document required the check before any P0 ΔΔG was reported; it has been made.

### Q3. Is the ordering consistent across protonation states?

**No. It inverts on deprotonation.** Two distinct orderings across the three states:

```
  P0:  Pb > Zn > Cu
  P1:  Cu > Pb > Zn
  P2:  Cu > Pb > Zn
```

| relation | P0 | P1 | P2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pb over Cu | yes | **NO** | **NO** |
| Pb over Zn | yes | yes | yes |

`DFT_PROTOCOL.md` §1.3 made this the sensitivity check and pre-committed to the outcome: *"the
reported claim is the one that survives all three."* **Only Pb > Zn survives all three.** The
Pb-over-Cu claim does not.

This turns the protonation state from a modelling assumption into a **load-bearing variable**: which
metal the galloyl site prefers depends on whether the ligand is protonated. That makes attack **A32**
(the unsourced pK_a, ruling D-04) considerably more serious than it was, because the pK_a now
determines which of two contradictory answers the calculation gives. It cannot be dismissed on the
grounds that the three-state design makes the premise unnecessary.

## 7. THE Pb COORDINATION NUMBER AT DFT LEVEL

`python dft/analysis/pb_coordination.py` · exit 0 · **limitations material for §5.3, not a headline result**

```
[Pb(H2O)6]2+  +  2 H2O   ->   [Pb(H2O)8]2+       Δn = -2, 2 waters CONSUMED
```

| level of theory | term | value / kJ mol⁻¹ | favours |
|---|---|---|---|
| GFN2-xTB / ALPB(water) | ΔE, electronic only | **−75.80** | CN = 8 |
| PBE0-D3BJ / def2-TZVP / SMD | ΔE electronic (incl. SMD + D3BJ) | **−43.69** | CN = 8 |
| | ΔG_thermal (ZPE + thermal − TS, quasi-RRHO) | **+90.87** | |
| | ΔG_ss standard state | **+4.05** | |
| | **ΔG total** | **+51.23** | **CN = 6** |

**The GFN2-xTB preference for eight-coordinate lead does not survive at DFT with SMD solvation.**
The swing between the two levels is 127.0 kJ/mol.

**Where the reversal comes from.** The DFT *electronic* energy agrees with GFN2 in direction
(−43.7 kJ/mol, favouring CN = 8) at roughly half the magnitude. It is the **thermal and entropic
term, +90.9 kJ/mol**, that reverses the preference: binding two additional waters into the cluster
costs their translational and rotational entropy, and **the GFN2 screen compared electronic energies
only and could not see that cost.**

**Independent structural evidence for the same conclusion.** The eight-water cluster does not stay
eight-coordinate: two waters relax to 3.793 Å and 4.089 Å, leaving a **six-coordinate first shell**.
The optimiser reached the same answer as the free energy, from the geometry.

### What this means for ruling D-01 and attack A33

Ruling D-01 fixed CN = 6 for a controlled comparison and **conceded the GFN2 CN = 8 preference as a
limitation**, naming two pieces of future work: a DFT-level comparison of both coordination numbers,
and an assessment of which is favoured *in water* rather than in the gas phase. **Both have now been
done, and both support the ruling rather than qualifying it.**

`DFT_PROTOCOL.md` §2.2 and the A33 entry currently read as a concession — "the present study's own
screening indicates as much: at the GFN2-xTB level the eight-coordinate ion is favoured". **That
paragraph is now out of date and understates the project's position.** It should be rewritten to say
that the screening preference was tested at the production level of theory in solution and did not
survive, by 51.2 kJ/mol. A33 moves from a limitation the report concedes to a question the report
answers.

**The caveat that remains** is that lead's *first shell* is measured at five in `pb_aquo6` and six in
`pb_aquo8`, so the model does not describe a clean six-coordinate lead aquo ion either. That belongs
in §5.3 and is a real limitation, distinct from the CN = 8 one.
