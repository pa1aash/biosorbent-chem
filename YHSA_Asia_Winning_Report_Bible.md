## SECTION I — INTRODUCTION (6–8 pages)

Purpose: establish that the *chemical* question is real, unsolved, and worth a Chemistry panel's attention; demonstrate literature thoroughness (C6); and stake the novelty claim (C2).

### 1.1 The problem: trace Pb(II) in mixed-metal aqueous streams (0.75–1 page)
- Scale and toxicity framing, kept short and cited. Two paragraphs maximum.
- Why *selective recovery* rather than bulk removal: recovery enables valorisation, reduces sludge, and permits reuse — this is where the 11–14× concentration factor becomes meaningful.
- Name the real streams: battery recycling leachate, e-waste, mine drainage, electroplating rinse. Give real concentration ranges with citations.

### 1.2 Why conventional biosorbents fail: the Irving–Williams trap (1–1.5 pages)
- State the Irving–Williams series explicitly and cite Irving & Williams (1953).
- Explain the mechanism of the trap: finite O-donor sites, higher-stability transition-metal complexes outcompete for them, so a non-specific sorbent is *thermodynamically steered away* from Pb(II).
- **Critical framing move:** Pb(II) sits *outside* the classical series because it is not a first-row d-block ion. Therefore selectivity for Pb(II) is not a matter of beating Irving–Williams on its own terms, but of engineering a site whose **selection rules are different** — geometry, polarisability, covalency and desolvation rather than charge density.
- This paragraph is the conceptual seed of the whole paper. Write it three times until it is airtight.

### 1.3 What is already known (2–2.5 pages) — the literature review proper
Organise as four explicit strands, each ending with a "gap" sentence:

**(a) Polyphenol/tannin sorbents for divalent metals.** Şengil & Özacar's valonia tannin resin and comparable systems; typical capacities; typical selectivity ordering. *Gap: selectivity is reported, seldom explained at the electronic-structure level.*

**(b) Collagen/ossein and protein-scaffold sorbents.** Fish-scale collagen isolation and thermal stability (Pati et al.); why a demineralised collagen scaffold is attractive (abundant carboxylate/amide, fibrous, waste-derived, low cost). *Gap: unfunctionalised collagen is non-selective.*

**(c) Pb(II) coordination chemistry and the stereochemically active 6s² lone pair.** Shimoni-Livny, Glusker & Bock on holodirected vs hemidirected geometries; the void-hemisphere concept; when hemidirection is favoured (low coordination number, soft/polarisable donors, anionic ligands). *Gap: hemidirection is well documented in crystallography, rarely used as a **design principle** for a sorbent.*

**(d) Computational treatment of metal–polyphenol binding.** DFT-D3, implicit solvation (SMD), EDA and NOCV approaches; what they have and have not been applied to. *Gap: no matched experimental-competitive/EDA study for a designed galloyl pocket across Pb/Cu/Zn.*

### 1.4 Hypothesis, aim and original contribution (1 page)
Write this as a hard-edged, boxed block:

> **Hypothesis.** A vicinal-trihydroxy (galloyl) oxygen-donor pocket immobilised on a collagen scaffold will invert the expected Irving–Williams ordering in favour of Pb(II), because (i) the pocket accommodates the stereochemically active 6s² lone pair in a hemidirected arrangement, (ii) donation into the diffuse Pb 6p manifold provides a covalent channel unavailable to the harder, more contracted Cu(II) and Zn(II) acceptors, and (iii) Pb(II)'s markedly lower hydration free energy imposes a smaller desolvation penalty on complexation.
>
> **Aim.** To test this hypothesis by matched experiment and first-principles calculation, and to determine whether the observed selectivity is electrostatically or covalently/desolvation controlled.
>
> **Original contributions of this work.**
> 1. Synthesis and full characterisation of a galloyl-functionalised ossein biosorbent from two waste streams.
> 2. Quantitative demonstration of Pb(II) selectivity under a deliberately adverse molar ratio (Pb at >3-fold molar deficit).
> 3. A DFT model that reproduces the experimental ordering without fitting.
> 4. **An energy decomposition that falsifies the purely electrostatic rationale** and localises the selectivity to orbital covalency and desolvation.
> 5. Demonstration that the mechanistic finding translates to a working, regenerable fixed-bed process with an 11–14× concentration factor.

### 1.5 Structure of this report (0.25 page)
One short paragraph mapping the sections. Winners do this; it costs three sentences and orients the referee.

## SECTION III — COMPUTATIONAL METHODOLOGY (4–5 pages)

Purpose: reproducibility, and pre-empting the "did a program produce this or did you?" question. **This section is where a chemistry panel with a computational referee will probe hardest.**

### 3.1 Model construction
- Justify the **cluster model**: which fragment of the galloyl–ossein represents the binding pocket, how it was truncated, how dangling bonds were capped, and why this truncation is defensible.
- Show the model as a **figure with atom labels**.
- Explicitly state the **charge and multiplicity** of every species. Cu(II) is d⁹ — **open shell, doublet**. If you treated it closed-shell, the entire computational arm is invalid. State the spin state and, for Cu, report ⟨S²⟩ and comment on spin contamination.
- Deprotonation state of the galloyl OH groups at pH 5 — this is a major determinant of ΔG_bind and must be justified, ideally with computed pKa or a stated assumption plus a sensitivity check.

### 3.2 Level of theory
- Functional and dispersion correction (e.g. ωB97X-D or B3LYP-D3(BJ)) — **and why that functional for a system containing a heavy post-transition metal**.
- Basis sets: light atoms; **effective core potential for Pb** (def2-TZVP with def2-ECP, or SDD) — the ECP must be named. Scalar-relativistic treatment must be stated. A Pb calculation without relativistic ECP or ZORA/DKH is a fatal, easily spotted flaw.
- Solvation: SMD, solvent = water, cite Marenich, Cramer & Truhlar.
- Software and **version number** (ORCA x.y.z), citing Neese.
- Convergence criteria, integration grid, whether frequencies were computed.

### 3.3 Thermochemistry
- How ΔG°_bind was constructed: gas-phase electronic energy + thermal/entropic corrections at 298.15 K + solvation free energy; state whether a 1 M → 1 M standard-state correction (1.89 kcal/mol) was applied.
- **State whether the reaction is written as ligand exchange with the aquo complex** — i.e. [M(H₂O)ₙ]²⁺ + L → [ML(H₂O)ₙ₋ₓ]²⁺ + xH₂O — rather than naked ion + ligand. **Naked-ion binding energies are physically meaningless and a referee will say so.** If your quoted ΔG°_bind values (−145.2, −110.4, −85.6 kJ/mol) came from a naked-ion reference, recast them as exchange free energies or clearly label them as reference-state-dependent.
- All frequencies real (no imaginary modes) → confirm minima.
- BSSE: counterpoise correction applied or an argument for why it is negligible.

### 3.4 Energy decomposition and NOCV
- **Name the exact scheme and the exact software.** ETS-NOCV is implemented in ADF; ORCA offers alternative decompositions (e.g. LED for DLPNO-CCSD(T), or interfaces to Multiwfn/NBO for charge-transfer analysis). **If you ran ORCA, you cannot describe the output as "ETS-NOCV" without stating the actual implementation route.** Resolve this before submission — it is the most likely single point of failure for a computational referee and the most likely oral-defence kill shot.
- Define the fragmentation used for the decomposition (metal fragment vs ligand fragment; fragment charges and spin states).
- Define f_orb explicitly: f_orb = ΔE_orb / (ΔE_elstat + ΔE_orb). State that this is a **ratio of decomposition terms and is scheme-dependent**, so comparisons are internally valid across your three metals but not directly transferable across studies. Saying this yourself is a rigour signal.

### 3.5 Geometric descriptors of hemidirection
Do not assert hemidirection — **measure it**. Provide at least one quantitative descriptor:
- Angle subtended by the ligand-donor void hemisphere, or
- The magnitude of the displacement of Pb from the centroid of its donor set, or
- The Λ / directionality parameter after Shimoni-Livny et al.
Tabulate the descriptor for Pb, Cu, Zn. A number here converts a qualitative claim into evidence.

### 3.6 Validation of the computational protocol
Compute one or two quantities with known experimental values (e.g. reproduce a literature hydration free energy trend, or a known Pb–O bond length in a small crystallographic complex) and report the deviation. This is short, cheap, and enormously credibility-generating.

# 12. WRITING STYLE, VOICE, TENSE AND TERMINOLOGY

- **Voice.** First person plural is standard and used by all four reference reports ("we prepared", "our catalyst"). Single-author reports still use "we" or shift to passive; either is fine, be consistent. You are a single-member team — "we" reads oddly for one person; prefer measured passive for methods ("The scales were demineralised…") and first-person singular sparingly, or "this work" / "the present study" for claims.
- **Tense.** Methods and results in past tense. Established facts and interpretations in present tense. Conclusions in present.
- **Hedging calibration.** Under-hedging kills credibility; over-hedging kills impact. Rule: **state findings assertively; state interpretations with a stated confidence basis.** "The sorbent captured 71.6 ± x% of Pb(II)" (assertive fact). "This is most consistent with a desolvation-controlled mechanism, since …" (interpretation with reason).
- **Never write "significantly" without a statistical test.** Use "markedly", "substantially" if you have no test; use "significantly (p = …)" if you do.
- **Numbers.** Consistent significant figures throughout; a capacity of 40.11 mg/g implies 4 s.f., which requires the measurement to support it. If your replicate SD is ±2 mg/g, report **40.1 ± 2.0**. Over-precise numbers are a classic tell of unexamined output.
- **Units.** SI throughout; mg/g *and* mmol/g for capacities; kJ/mol for energies (be consistent — do not mix kcal/mol from software output).
- **Terminology precision.** Biosorbent, sorption, sorbate/sorbent; "adsorption" only if you have evidence it is surface-confined; "complexation" for the coordination event; "uptake" as the neutral term. Do not say "absorption."
- **Abbreviations.** Define at first use, then use consistently. Provide an abbreviation list in the front matter if you exceed ~15.
- **Do not use rhetorical questions, exclamation marks, or promotional adjectives** ("remarkable", "revolutionary", "unprecedented"). Chem-049 says "promising" and "comparable"; it never says "revolutionary." Restraint is a status signal.
- **Sentence length.** Methods: short and declarative. Discussion: allow longer sentences for causal chains, but never more than two clauses of subordination.
- **Consistency audit before submission:** search the document for every headline number (40.11, 25.38, 16.86, 71.6, 41.0, 26.4, 3.63, 7.03, 45, 11–14, 80.1, −145.2, −110.4, −85.6, 0.38, +15.2, +136.5) and confirm each appears identically in abstract, results, and conclusion. Mismatched numbers across sections are the fastest way to lose a referee's trust.

# 18. ANTI-PATTERNS — THE TWELVE THINGS THAT KILL REPORTS

1. **Framing as engineering, not chemistry.** The Chemistry panel is not the Environmental Science panel.
2. **A results section that describes figures instead of arguing from them.** Every subsection must end in a verdict.
3. **Over-precise numbers with no uncertainty.** 40.11 mg/g without a ± is a claim you cannot defend.
4. **Linearised isotherm fits** — the single most cited methodological error in the sorption literature, and you have already cited the paper that says so.
5. **Naked-ion or unspecified-reference-state binding energies.**
6. **Asserting hemidirection instead of measuring it.**
7. **Hiding the computed-vs-experimental magnitude discrepancy.** Referees find it. Pre-empt it and it becomes a strength.
8. **A thin reference list.** 10 references is an outline; 45 is a report.
9. **No control sorbent, or a control mentioned but not plotted alongside.** Your α control values are among your strongest data — put them *on the same axes*.
10. **No failed experiments reported.** Reads as either lucky or synthetic.
11. **Vague AI declaration.** In 2026 this is a live disqualification vector, not a formality.
12. **Missing principal signature / wrong filename / two files instead of one / emailed >5 MB attachment.** Losing on formatting after doing real science is the worst possible outcome. Section 2 is a checklist; treat it as one.
